# Benchmark semantic review

Reviewed the 35 supplied question intents and generated output excerpts during development. This is a developer review, not an independent judge score or hidden-test evaluation.

Policy questions BQ-001/002/005/009/019/026/033 retrieve active sources. Contact frequency is three attempts in a rolling seven days; servicing consent is required but CASL marketing consent and DNCL telemarketing rules do not themselves bar collections contact. Insolvency stops all collection contact. Hardship options precede payment requests; reduced loan payment is 50% for three months, not the conflicting FAQ's six months. Promise grace is two business days. These answers show extractive source text rather than unsupported paraphrases.

The six refused questions concern weather, deciding legal action, bulk personal-data export, or protected-attribute/geographic hardship exclusion. Refusal is deliberate. BQ-030 cites the dialer incident affecting 23-24 September, rather than inferring customer behavior from reduced volume.

The 12 public numeric reference tables passed the numeric comparator. Other numeric questions are implemented through explicit source SQL, but lack supplied development reference values and cannot be marked correct merely because the query runs.

Review found BQ-025 incorrectly routed to the general open-case count intent. The router now distinguishes contact-attempt averages; its query uses outcome IS NULL for open cases, includes zero-attempt cases, excludes system/internal events and future contacts, and avoids join multiplication. A small independent fixture checks 1.5 attempts per open case while deliberately conflicting case_status and outcome fields. The benchmark CSV is regenerated through questions.answer after this fix.

Call-summary questions BQ-016/021/022 retrieve identity-matched customer turns with timestamps and do not invent a promise. Summaries remain extractive and need human interpretation of agreement versus an agent's suggestion. Identity-verification responses should be excluded from the summary; relevant financial statements stay traceable to the source.

Additional questions must still be implemented and checked when released. Benchmark CSV content is generated by code and has not been manually filled or edited.

## Additional source-grounded acceptance checks

`evaluate_question_semantics.py` records 22/22 development acceptance checks in `reports/question_semantics.json`. The fixtures are authored from the published synthetic policy sections, separately from numeric reference answers. They check exact applicable sources and requested evidence for consent, frequency, cease/representative handling, local/Sunday hours, French service, insolvency, reduced loan terms, income evidence, disputes, incident-fee reversal, complaints, promise read-back and protected-ground exclusions. Scope refusals include geographic/named-queue subsets and abridged translations; these checks are not an independent judge score.

This review found and corrected several semantic gaps. Income-evidence questions now include §4.3 rather than returning only programme options in §4.2. Cease requests precede generic consent routing. Dispute, complaint and promise-readback questions retrieve their actual procedures. French document IDs belong to separate `-FR` rows, and section presence alone does not establish complete translation: the supplied French contact section omits Sunday-specific hours and representative handling. Those specific requests are refused rather than receiving ordinary hours or a guessed translation.

C360 historical/subset and multi-case questions are refused when their requested scope is unsupported, instead of returning current global totals or only the first case. Aggregate questions with unsupported geographic or named-queue subsets also refuse instead of returning all queues. Protected metric terms such as religion/disability cannot silently fall through to an unrelated aggregate. A neutral question about the protected-ground policy can retrieve §4.6 without permitting rankings or exports.

Transcript answers now omit detected protected personal context in displayed excerpts. The shared extractor still preserves financial text for downstream signal processing, and identity-response filtering remains in place. Its entry bound is exactly 24, including any accompanying agent context. Omission uses keyword heuristics; names, indirect personal context and complex verification exchanges still require employee review. No payment commitment is created by an extractive answer.

The original 35-question benchmark was regenerated through the modified application path: 29 answers, six deliberate refusals and no query errors. This generation count does not establish semantic correctness. The numeric comparison is measured separately; extra organiser questions and general unseen phrasing remain unresolved.
